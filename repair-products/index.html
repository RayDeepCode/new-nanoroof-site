Roof repair products

The professional-grade repair products a contractor uses in the tune-up to close the places shingles can’t protect: flashings, joints, penetrations and cracks.

See the 10-point tune-upTechnical data
Illustration

The repair line

Two products, each for a different kind of joint.

Caulk

Elastomeric caulk

A flexible caulk for gaps, flashings, shingles and roof penetrations.

  • Adheres to: masonry, wood, asphalt, metal and vinyl
  • Conditions: can go on damp surfaces and in low temperatures
  • Typical uses: exposed nail heads, lifted tabs, pipe boots, step flashing
Liquid flashing

Liquid flashing

A clear, brushable elastomeric caulk for the joints where water gets in.

  • On the roof: chimneys, valleys, vents, flashing and gutters
  • Around the house: sills, thresholds and siding
  • Applied: by brush, so it reaches into seams and laps

Where they go on a roof

Most leaks start where the roof meets something else.

Chimneys and walls

Step and counter flashing, where shingles meet masonry or siding.

Valleys

Where two slopes meet and carry the most water.

Vents and pipe boots

Plumbing vents, exhaust fans and their rubber boots.

Skylights

The perimeter flashing around the frame.

Nail heads and tabs

Exposed fasteners and lifted shingle tabs.

Gutters and edges

Seams, end caps and drip edge joints.

How they’re used

  • In every tune-up: repairs come before any preservation treatment
  • Recorded with photos: each repair goes into the Roof Record, before and after
  • Priced separately: repairs found at the assessment are listed and priced on their own

Who can buy them

  • Professional-grade: sold only to Certified Contractors, not to homeowners or at retail
  • Data sheets: available on request
  • Opening order: a case of each comes with a new contractor’s opening order
