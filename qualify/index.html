Does your roof qualify?

Preservation only works on a roof that is still sound. Every job starts with a Roof-Life Assessment, and some roofs don't pass it.

Illustration

Who it’s for, and who it isn’t

Preservation only works on a roof that is still sound. Every job starts with a Roof-Life Assessment, and some roofs don't pass it.

Usually a good fit

  • Asphalt shingle roofs showing age: fading, brittleness, early granule loss
  • Roofs past their first several years, once the factory surface has worn in
  • Algae or moss streaking that has been washed off first
  • Homeowners, landlords and property managers who want to delay a replacement

We'll tell you no if

  • The decking is soft or the roof has structural damage
  • Granule loss has gone too far for the shingles to be saved
  • The roof is so new that it doesn't need treatment yet
  • The real problem is a flashing leak. Leaks are repaired first; preservation doesn't fix them

Roof age is a guide, not the answer

Condition decides, but age tells you when to look.

Under 6-8 years (southern climates) or 8-10 years (northern)Usually too early. New shingles have a factory surface that has to wear in before the material can bond. A dealer can check and tell you when.
Roughly 8-20 yearsThe usual window, once fading, brittleness or early granule loss appear and the decking is sound.
Over 20 yearsSometimes still qualifies. It depends on granule loss, brittleness and the decking, so the assessment answers it.

Ranges are general guidance from field experience, not a rule. The assessment is what qualifies a roof.
