Does treating a roof trap moisture?

A common worry: if you seal the shingles, will water get stuck underneath? Here’s how the SPS System behaves—and what dealers measure first.

Start with moisture readingsWhy silicone
Illustration

The claim you’ll hear“If you coat or treat a shingle roof, you’ll trap moisture and rot the deck.”

What that claim assumes

It assumes a film that seals like a tarp over wet wood. NANOROOF isn’t that kind of product, and the protocol doesn’t spray a wet roof blind.

  1. It’s not a plastic tarpSPS penetrates and bonds inside the asphalt and adds a thin encapsulating layer. It is built to shed liquid water at the surface—not to create a sealed tank above wet insulation.
  2. Moisture is measured firstBaseline readings go on the Roof Record before spraying. The point is to know the starting condition, not to pretend every roof is bone-dry.
  3. The whole roof still mattersSoft decking, blocked ventilation and active leaks are assessment failures or repair-first items. Treatment doesn’t “fix” a wet structure.

How dealers think about moisture

Three practical distinctions—liquid water, vapor, and a failed assembly.

  1. 1
    Liquid water on the roof

    Rain should shed. Encapsulation helps water bead and run to the gutters instead of soaking into weathered asphalt.

  2. 2
    Vapor in the attic

    Attics still need to breathe. Preservation doesn’t replace ventilation, insulation or a dry deck.

  3. 3
    A roof that is already wet inside

    If the deck is soft or saturated, the honest answer is repair or replace—not spray and hope.
