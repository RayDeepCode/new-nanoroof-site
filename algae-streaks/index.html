Dark streaks on your roof?

Black streaks are usually algae, and green clumps are moss. Both are fixable, but moss in particular holds water against the shingle and lifts it.

Free Roof-Life Assessment
Illustration
Roof problemsGranule lossAlgae and mossCurling and crackingRoof leaks

What you’re seeing

  • Black or dark streaks: Usually algae, most often on shaded, north-facing slopes
  • Green clumps: Moss, often along the lower edges and in shaded areas
  • Crusty patches: Lichen, which grips granules and can pull them loose
  • From the ground: check the shaded slopes and lower edges first, where growth takes hold

What causes it

Algae and moss feed on moisture and the materials in shingles. Shade and humidity help them spread, and moss holds water against the roof long after rain stops.

Left alone, growth works granules loose. It also sits between the shingle and anything applied over it, and keeps feeding underneath.

Can preservation help?

Usually a good candidate

Growth comes off with a low-pressure soft wash, without stripping granules. Once the roof is clean and sound, the SPS System encapsulates it.

  1. Don’t pressure washHigh pressure strips the granules the roof depends on
  2. Soft wash firstroof cleaner at low pressure, then a gentle rinse
  3. Then preserveBaseline readings, then the SPS System

When cleaning and preservation aren’t enough

Some roofs need more than a wash.

  • Too much granule loss: if the shingles have lost too many granules, they can’t be saved
  • Soft decking or structural damage: that means replacement
  • A leak under the growth: leaks are repaired first in the tune-up; preservation doesn’t fix them
